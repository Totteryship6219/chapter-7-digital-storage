Section 02 • Core Concepts

Digital Storage Fundamentals

Digital storage (also known as secondary storage) holds data, instructions, and information permanently for future use. Unlike RAM, which is volatile and loses its contents when power is turned off, storage devices are non-volatile.

How Storage Is Measured (Capacity Hierarchy)

Computers process data as binary digits (bits: 0 or 1). Eight bits group together to form one Byte—enough to store a single character like the letter 'A'.

1 Kilobyte (KB) ~1,024 Bytes (Short email)
1 Megabyte (MB) ~1,024 KB (1 MP3 song)
1 Gigabyte (GB) ~1,024 MB (HD movie)
1 Terabyte (TB) ~1,024 GB (250,000 photos)
1 Petabyte (PB) ~1,024 TB (Cloud cluster)
1 Exabyte (EB) ~1,024 PB (Global data)

5 Key Storage Metrics

1. Capacity Total amount of data a medium can hold (GB/TB)
2. Speed (Access Time) Read/write throughput (MB/s) & latency (ms)
3. Durability Resistance to drops, magnets, heat & wear
4. Portability Physical size, weight & plug-and-play ease
5. Cost per GB Economic efficiency ($/GB or $/TB)
Magnetic Storage

1. Hard Disk Drive (HDD)

Uses rapidly spinning magnetic platters (5,400 or 7,200 RPM) and a moving read/write actuator head that floats nanometers above the surface.

  • Pros: Lowest cost per TB ($0.02/GB); capacities up to 24 TB+.
  • Cons: Mechanical moving parts are vulnerable to drops; slower speeds (~80–160 MB/s); audible noise.
Speed: ~150 MB/s Best For: Bulk Archiving & NAS
Fastest Primary
Solid-State NAND

2. SATA SSD & NVMe SSD

Stores data electronically on non-volatile NAND flash memory chips with zero moving parts.

SATA III SSD (2.5"): Up to 550 MB/s; great for upgrading older laptops.
NVMe M.2 SSD (PCIe): Connects directly to CPU PCIe lanes for 3,500–7,400+ MB/s speeds!
  • Pros: Instant boot times, silent, shock-resistant, energy-efficient.
  • Cons: Higher cost per GB than HDD; finite write cycles (TBW).
Speed: 550–7,400 MB/s Best For: OS, Gaming, 4K Editing
Portable Flash

3. USB Flash Drives

Pocket-sized solid-state storage (also called thumb drives) that plug directly into USB-A or USB-C ports for instant file transfers.

  • Pros: Ultra-portable, plug-and-play across OS platforms, durable.
  • Cons: Easy to misplace or lose; security risk if unencrypted.
Capacity: 16 GB – 2 TB Best For: School & Quick Transfers
Removable Flash

4. Memory Cards (SD / microSD)

Postage-stamp or fingernail-sized flash cards (SDHC, SDXC, CFexpress) engineered for digital cameras, drones, smartphones, and handheld consoles.

  • Pros: Tiny form factor, waterproof/shockproof ratings, replaceable.
  • Cons: Requires card reader on many PCs; fragile connectors if bent.
Speed: 90–300 MB/s (UHS) Best For: Cameras, Drones, Switch
Laser Optical

5. Optical: CD, DVD & Blu-ray

Uses laser beams to read microscopic pits (0s) and lands (1s) etched onto polycarbonate discs.

CD: 700 MB (Red laser, 780nm)
DVD: 4.7 GB single / 8.5 GB dual-layer
Blu-ray (BD): 25 GB – 100 GB (Blue-violet laser, 405nm)
  • Pros: Immune to magnetic fields; write-once (ROM/R) prevents ransomware tampering.
  • Cons: Prone to scratches; slow speeds; rare drives on modern laptops.
Capacity: 700 MB – 100 GB Best For: Media & Cold Archiving
Remote / Network

6. Cloud Storage

Stores data on remote server arrays inside massive data centers (Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud, AWS S3) accessed over the internet.

  • Pros: Accessible anywhere, automatic sync & collaboration, off-site disaster protection.
  • Cons: Requires internet connection; recurring monthly subscription fees; privacy considerations.
Capacity: Elastic / Unlimited Best For: Sync, Collaboration, Backup
Disaster Recovery

The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

Hardware always fails eventually. To guarantee zero data loss from drive crashes, theft, or ransomware:

3 Copies of Data: 1 primary working copy + 2 backups.
2 Different Media: E.g., internal NVMe SSD + external HDD/NAS.
1 Off-Site Copy: Stored in Cloud Storage or a separate physical building.
Cryptography

Data Encryption

Encryption scrambles readable plaintext into unreadable ciphertext using mathematical algorithms and a secret key.

  • Full Disk Encryption (FDE): Tools like Windows BitLocker and macOS FileVault use AES-256 to protect entire drives at rest.
  • In-Transit vs. At-Rest: Cloud storage encrypts files via TLS while uploading (in-transit) and AES-256 on server disks (at-rest).
Information Security

Lifecycle & Sanitization

Simply pressing "Delete" or emptying the Recycle Bin only removes the file pointer—the actual data bits remain recoverable until overwritten!

  • Secure Wipe / Cryptographic Erase: Overwrites all sectors with zeros/random bits or destroys the SSD's encryption key.
  • RAID Redundancy: Enterprises combine multiple drives (RAID 1 mirroring, RAID 5/6 striping with parity) for fault tolerance.

ASHRAE Server Room Environmental Standards

Enterprise storage arrays operate best between 18°C and 27°C (64°F–80°F) with 40%–60% relative humidity. Too much humidity causes condensation corrosion; too little humidity increases electrostatic discharge (ESD) that can damage SSD controllers.
